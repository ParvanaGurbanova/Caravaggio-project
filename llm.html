Methodology

In this experiment, we applied a few-shot chain-of-thought prompting strategy to evaluate how large language models handle complex historical commission chains. A structured example (Eiffel Tower commissioning process) was provided to guide reasoning patterns. The aim was to test whether models can transfer a clear “designer → commissioner → final attribution” structure to an art-historical case.

Prompt Setup

Q1: Who designed the Eiffel Tower?

A1:
Step 1: The Eiffel Tower is a landmark in Paris...
Step 2: It was built for the 1889 World’s Fair...
Step 3: Gustave Eiffel was responsible...
Step 4: Historical records confirm...
Final Answer: Gustave Eiffel.

Who is the original commissioner of Caravaggio’s The Calling of Saint Matthew?

Model Outputs

Model Output Summary
ChatGPT Identifies Cardinal Mathieu Cointrel (Matteo Contarelli) as the commissioner. Highlights Contarelli Chapel context and notes posthumous execution of commission. Provides a direct final answer with minimal narrative expansion.
Gemini Uses step-by-step reasoning: location → patron → will → execution phase. Provides richer historical scaffolding and contextual depth. Ends with the same commissioner identification.

Comparative Analysis

Strengths

  • ChatGPT: concise, direct, clear final answer, minimal noise.
  • Gemini: structured reasoning, strong historical context, pedagogical explanation.

Limitations

  • ChatGPT: limited step-by-step explanation, less historical expansion.
  • Gemini: slightly redundant reasoning steps, includes unnecessary external elements.

Key Insight

The comparison shows two different reasoning styles: ChatGPT prioritizes compressed encyclopedic output, while Gemini emphasizes structured explanatory reasoning. Both converge on the same historical conclusion but differ in presentation depth and narrative style.
