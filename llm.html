1. Experiment Overview

In this experiment, we applied a few-shot chain-of-thought prompting strategy to evaluate how large language models handle historical commission reasoning. A structured example (Eiffel Tower commissioning process) was used to guide reasoning patterns.

The goal was to test whether models could transfer a structured “designer → commissioner → final attribution” reasoning model to Caravaggio’s The Calling of Saint Matthew.

2. Prompt Structure

Example Task: Who designed the Eiffel Tower?

Step 1: Built for 1889 World’s Fair
Step 2: Entrance arch structure
Step 3: Gustave Eiffel’s company
Step 4: Gustave Eiffel is the designer

Final Answer: Gustave Eiffel

Target Question:
Who is the original commissioner of Caravaggio’s The Calling of Saint Matthew?

3. Model Outputs

Model Response
ChatGPT Directly identifies Cardinal Mathieu Cointrel (Matteo Contarelli) as commissioner. Mentions Contarelli Chapel and estate execution context.
Gemini Uses step-by-step reasoning: location → patron → will → posthumous execution. More detailed historical breakdown.

4. Comparative Analysis

Model Strengths Limitations
ChatGPT Direct, concise, accurate final answer, minimal noise Lacks explicit step-by-step reasoning structure
Gemini Strong chronological explanation, pedagogical clarity More verbose, slightly redundant steps, includes extra content

5. Key Insight

ChatGPT follows a compressed encyclopedic reasoning style, focusing on direct answers.

Gemini follows a structured pedagogical reasoning style, emphasizing step-by-step explanation.

Both models converge on the same conclusion: Cardinal Mathieu Cointrel (Matteo Contarelli).
