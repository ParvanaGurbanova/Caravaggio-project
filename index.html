The Calling of Saint Matthew

One of Caravaggio's most celebrated masterpieces, depicting the biblical moment when Christ calls Matthew to become one of his apostles. The painting is renowned for its dramatic use of light, realism, and symbolic depth.

Project Introduction

For this project, we decided to work with Caravaggio’s painting “The Calling of Saint Matthew” (1599-1600), one of the defining masterpieces of Baroque art. Commissioned for the Contarelli Chapel in the church of San Luigi dei Francesi in Rome, this painting is renowned for its innovative use of chiaroscuro, naturalistic style, and powerful depiction of the biblical moment when Christ calls Matthew to be one of his apostles.

We decided to focus on this painting because of its profound cultural, historic and religious importance and because of the wealth of symbolic connotations that makes it suitable to represent them semantically. By choosing such an influential painting, we aimed to explore how knowledge graphs can reflect not only factual information such as authorship, location, and historical context, but also relationships that contribute to a more comprehensive description of the artwork.

In the context of the Knowledge Engineering for the Humanities (KE4H) course, our goal was to enrich the current cultural heritage knowledge graph with additional information by employing SPARQL queries and Large Language Models (LLMs). In particular, we increased the available information by adding details about who commissioned the painting and we included English labels and descriptions in addition to the existing Italian ones, making the resource more complete and accessible for multilingual querying and international users.
